Most racing games give you money for finishing first. PotHole Dodgers gives you something better: **the wreck itself is the reward.** The economy runs on three resources, and each one answers a different question about how you play.

## SCRAP — the common currency

Scrap comes from wrecked vehicles and event debris. It's everywhere, it's cheap, and it buys parts. If you're poor in this game, the fix is simple: drive somewhere there are more cars, and make them wrecks. Scrap is what keeps the free-roam loop alive — even when you can't afford to enter an event, the world is literally paying you just for being violent.

## FLUID — the premium resource

Fluid is earned from event placement and difficulty, not from raw destruction. It buys upgrades, premium fuel, and black market access. This is the "did you actually play well" currency. You can scavenge scrap forever, but a tuned car takes fluid — and fluid takes racing.

## AMMO — rare, black market only

Ammo never appears in normal drops. Light AP, Heavy HE, EMP charges — all of it comes from the black market, which means it's priced by demand and gated behind FLUID. Combat capability is a luxury, not a default. A player who wants to be a tank has to race to afford it.

## Dynamic pricing

Prices aren't static. They fluctuate based on recent event density and market activity — raid events spike ammo demand, which pushes prices up, which changes what's worth raiding next. The economy reacts to the world's own rhythm.

The design goal underneath all of this: **no two playstyles should be equally viable.** A pure racer, a scavenger, and a black-market gunner should each feel like they're playing a different game with the same car keys.
