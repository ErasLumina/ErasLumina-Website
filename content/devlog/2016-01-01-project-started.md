Every studio has an origin story. Ours starts with traffic.

Around 2015, I was staying in Boksburg and commuting through Gauteng road chaos every day — everyone doing what they wanted, causing delays, the highway grinding to a crawl. One thought kept coming back: *how nice would it be to just blast these cars out of the way.* Silly, maybe. But if it works, it's not dumb.

That thought became a game design document, written in 2016. I still have it.

The first implementation attempt was Unity with C#. It died almost immediately — I was already working in C++, and I knew Unreal could do the region-blocking (terrain chunking) this project would need. So the project moved to Unreal right away.

Then came the long gap. A GTX 960 with 2 GB of VRAM couldn't handle it, and I kept missing the window for a GTX 1080. On top of that, the Windows era was brutal — constant blue screens, three or four hard drives lost, the PC itself failing. The project had to be restarted so many times I stopped counting.

In 2026, after migrating the whole studio to Linux, development finally got back on rails. A decade-old GDD, a working engine, and a machine that doesn't fall over. That's where this devlog picks up.
